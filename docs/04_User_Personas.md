# Persona 1 — Business Owner

## Name

David Mwangi

## Age

42

## Occupation

Owner of a Digital Marketing Company

## Team Size

35 Employees

## Technical Experience

Intermediate. Comfortable with desktop software and web applications. Uses tools like Google Workspace, Asana, and QuickBooks. Not a power user but expects systems to be intuitive.

## Goals

- Monitor overall business productivity across all teams.
- Ensure projects finish on time and within budget.
- Reduce missed deadlines and improve on-time delivery rate.
- Improve employee accountability without micromanaging.
- Make data-driven decisions about resource allocation.

## Frustrations

- Doesn't know real-time project status without chasing managers.
- Too many status update meetings that could be replaced by self-service reporting.
- Poor visibility into who is working on what and whether work is blocked.
- Delayed customer deliveries due to lack of visibility.
- Difficulty measuring individual and team productivity objectively.

## Success Looks Like

Opening one dashboard in the morning and immediately knowing the health of the business: which projects are at risk, which teams are overloaded, and where intervention is needed. Able to export a report for a board meeting in under 2 minutes.

## Behaviors and Context

- Checks dashboard 2-3 times per day, primarily in the morning and before meetings.
- Delegates task creation and day-to-day management to managers but needs roll-up visibility.
- Frequently asks managers for status updates in weekly meetings; wants to reduce this through self-service reporting.
- Values time highly; will abandon tools that require extensive training or configuration.
- Makes hiring and firing decisions based on productivity data.
- Uses a desktop computer (Windows) and occasionally a tablet for travel.

# Persona 2 — Project Manager

## Name

Grace Wanjiku

## Age

34

## Occupation

Project Manager

## Team Size

Manages 3 project teams (12 people total)

## Technical Experience

Advanced. Uses Jira, Trello, Slack, and Microsoft Project regularly. Comfortable with web applications and expects power-user features like bulk actions, keyboard shortcuts, and integrations.

## Goals

- Create projects and break them into actionable tasks.
- Assign tasks to the right team members based on skills and capacity.
- Monitor team progress in real time.
- Meet deadlines and deliver projects on time.
- Reduce administrative overhead of status tracking.
- Generate reports for stakeholders.

## Frustrations

- Team members forget tasks or claim they were never assigned.
- Difficult to follow up on overdue tasks across multiple communication channels.
- Progress is scattered across WhatsApp, email, and verbal updates.
- No single source of truth for project status.
- Time wasted in status meetings that could be replaced by dashboard reviews.
- Unable to quickly identify who is blocked and needs help.

## Success Looks Like

Every project has clear ownership, deadlines, and progress tracking visible in one place. Team members proactively update their task status. Reports are available in seconds rather than hours of manual compilation.

## Behaviors and Context

- Spends 30-60 minutes daily updating and reviewing task status.
- Creates tasks in bulk during project planning sessions.
- Uses the application on a desktop during work hours.
- Sends status reports to the business owner weekly; wants to automate this with exports.
- Frequently reassigns tasks when team members are overloaded or when priorities change.

# Persona 3 — Team Member

## Name

Brian Otieno

## Age

27

## Occupation

Software Developer

## Team Size

Works in a team of 5 developers

## Technical Experience

Advanced. Comfortable with development tools, APIs, and technical documentation. Prefers keyboard-driven interfaces and dislikes unnecessary clicks.

## Goals

- Know exactly what to work on today without asking the manager.
- Complete tasks efficiently and meet deadlines.
- Communicate blockers quickly to get unblocked.
- Track personal progress and productivity.

## Frustrations

- Confusing priorities when tasks are not clearly ranked.
- Constant interruptions asking for status updates.
- Missing information on task requirements, forcing back-and-forth communication.
- Tasks assigned verbally or via chat get lost or forgotten.
- Unable to see the bigger picture of how his work fits into the project.

## Success Looks Like

A simple dashboard showing exactly what to work on today, sorted by priority. Clear task descriptions with all necessary context. Ability to update status and flag blockers in under 10 seconds without leaving the application.

## Behaviors and Context

- Checks dashboard first thing in the morning and after lunch.
- Updates task status immediately when starting or completing work.
- Uses keyboard shortcuts heavily and gets frustrated with mouse-heavy interfaces.
- Frequently works offline or with poor internet; needs fast page loads.
- Values transparency and wants to see project timelines and dependencies (future feature).

# Persona 4 — System Administrator

## Name

Sarah Njeri

## Age

38

## Occupation

IT Administrator

## Team Size

Supports 200+ users across multiple organizations

## Technical Experience

Advanced. Manages cloud infrastructure, user directories, security policies, and compliance requirements. Comfortable with CLI tools and administrative interfaces.

## Goals

- Manage user accounts and access permissions securely.
- Configure system settings and security policies.
- Maintain system reliability and uptime.
- Monitor system health and respond to incidents.
- Ensure compliance with data protection regulations.

## Frustrations

- Manual user onboarding and offboarding is time-consuming.
- Lack of audit trails makes it difficult to trace security incidents.
- Fragmented user management across multiple tools.
- No centralized view of system health and user activity.
- Difficulty enforcing consistent security policies.

## Success Looks Like

The system remains secure, reliable, and easy to administer. User accounts can be created and deactivated in seconds. Audit logs provide complete traceability of all actions. System health is visible at a glance.

## Behaviors and Context

- Performs bulk user operations during onboarding cycles (monthly or quarterly).
- Monitors system logs and metrics weekly.
- Prefers bulk actions, filters, and export functionality.
- Works with multiple browser tabs and tools simultaneously.
- Needs clear error messages and detailed logging for troubleshooting.

# Persona 5 — Team Leader

## Name

James Kipchoge

## Age

45

## Occupation

Team Leader / Department Head

## Team Size

20 direct reports across 2 teams

## Technical Experience

Intermediate. Uses web applications for business operations but is not a power user. Prefers simple, guided interfaces over complex dashboards.

## Goals

- Create tasks for team members based on departmental objectives.
- Assign tasks and set clear expectations.
- Monitor team progress and identify bottlenecks.
- Ensure team meets departmental deadlines.
- Generate simple reports for senior management.

## Frustrations

- Unclear task priorities lead to team members working on low-value items.
- Lack of visibility into who is blocked and needs support.
- Difficulty tracking task completion rates across the team.
- Status updates are inconsistent and often delayed.
- Cannot easily see team capacity and workload distribution.

## Success Looks Like

A clear view of all team tasks, sorted by priority and due date. Ability to quickly see who is blocked and intervene. Simple reports showing team performance that can be shared in meetings without additional formatting.

## Behaviors and Context

- Creates tasks during weekly planning meetings.
- Checks dashboard at the start and end of each day.
- Prefers mobile-friendly or responsive interfaces for occasional on-the-go checking (post-V1).
- Generates weekly reports for the business owner.
- Uses the application on a desktop during work hours.

# Future Personas (Post-V1)

The following personas represent future user segments identified in the BRD. They are documented for roadmap planning but are not in scope for V1.

## Future Persona — Enterprise Organization

**Name:** Patricia Njoroge  
**Role:** VP of Operations, Enterprise Company (500+ employees)  
**Needs:** Advanced permissions, SSO, audit logs, compliance reporting, dedicated support, SLA guarantees.  
**Why Post-V1:** Enterprise features require significant additional development and operational infrastructure.

**Name:** Alex Mwende  
**Role:** Independent Freelancer  
**Needs:** Simple task tracking, client management, invoicing integration, mobile access.  
**Why Post-V1:** V1 is designed for team collaboration. Freelancer-specific features like client portals and invoicing are out of scope.

## Future Persona — Consultant

**Name:** Dr. Emily Wairimu  
**Role:** Management Consultant  
**Needs:** Project templates, multi-client workspaces, time tracking, expense tracking, client reporting.  
**Why Post-V1:** Consultant workflows require features like project templates, time tracking, and client billing that are deferred to post-V1.
